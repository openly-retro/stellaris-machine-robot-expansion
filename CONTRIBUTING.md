# Policy on AI contributions

Borrowing from QUEMU's [Code Provenance](https://github.com/qemu/qemu/blob/master/docs/devel/code-provenance.rst):

**The Machine & Robot Expansion: Continued project policy is to DECLINE any contributions which are believed to include or derive from AI generated content. This includes ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot, Llama and similar tools.**

*Note: If you go ahead and use an LLM to make code changes for this mod, and your pull request is declined for whatever reasons, I am sorry to say that we in this project cannot reimburse you for your tokens spent.*

There are a few reasons for this:

## 1 - Value people over machines*

Even though the theme of this mod is "Machines & Robots", behind the scenes it is real people in meat-space using their (valuable) free time and passion for Stellaris. Accepting contributions made by ChatGPT, etc, would mean that we don't really care about effort and only care about results. Learning to do modding for Stellaris is challenging but becomes very rewarding.

This is a mod for free, for the Stellaris community. It's not a business product so there is no mad rush to get things out the door, and there are no investors to keep happy by showing aggressive growth. The stakeholders for this project are the players, people who value quality and inventiveness. We do this for fun, and when it's not fun, we take a break.

## 2 - Modder does not learn the codebase by using LLMs

This is a large mod which I have been working on for over 2 years now, and I have rewritten a lot of it, so I know the ins and outs. Legit-Rikk has been a great contributor and he has become familiar with many parts of the mod during his time here.

I appreciate that people want to help, but if for example they don't know how to write Clausewitz script, or the mod is too large, and then LLM seems like a straightforward way to get involved.. that's not going to result in a modder who knows the codebase, much less one who knows about their own changes.

Also, software and code made by LLMs becomes much harder to maintain over time.

## 3 - LLMs don't 'know' how to code

The output is probabilistic, the LLMs create a solution based on statistical analysis of a very large amount of samples. Probabilistic meaning, given the same input, the output will not be always the same. There is no "knowledge" of code, only a map of what most likely comes after, for example, `icon = `.

## 4 - LLMs were not trained in an ethical way

This part is controversial and for me (Openly Retro) it is worth adding to the list of reasons. Though the first three reasons could be enough for most people, in these times now it's important to recognize that countless writers, artists, coders, thinkers, did not give their permission for all their works to be scraped by AI companies and used as training fodder for commercial products. Also, no framework for proper compensation has yet been created.

LLMs can do what they do because the companies violated (and still violate) the copyright and intellectual property of all those people whose works they take for free, without asking. Fair use does not cover this. For example, a person can pay for a LLM to create some art, and the person can make money selling that art, but the artists whose works were taken as training data for that new art do not receive any residuals or royalty payments as a result of the derivative works created, in part because the sources are untraceable.

# You can learn to mod by hand!

You can really do it. You don't need an LLM. You don't need to be perfectly rested or have enormous blocks of free time. There are people who do modding while overworked, sleep-deprived, and not at their best. Modding happens slowly over time.

There are a great amount of community resources on the topic. Stellaris Modding Den discord, Stellaris Modding Wiki, and there is a sub-reddit too.

When you look back on something you are good at now, there was a time you didn't know how to do it, and it seemed challenging. Learning happens over time, and is best done in a community. It's just the same with modding Stellaris, you can do it too! When things are difficult, you will have many people able to relate to you, and when it works, there will be people to share in the joy too.
